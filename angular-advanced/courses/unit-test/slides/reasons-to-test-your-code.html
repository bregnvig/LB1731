Bullet Point 1: "Identify bugs" Testing is crucial for finding bugs or errors before they reach the user. This helps prevent issues that could cause the software to malfunction, crash, or give incorrect results. Identifying bugs early in the development process can significantly improve stability and help avoid customer frustration. Share an example where a critical bug was found through testing that, if left unchecked, would have had a major impact. Bullet Point 2: "Ensure functionality" Testing ensures that the code behaves as expected and meets the requirements laid out during the design phase. It's not just about whether the software works but also if it works correctly according to the user's needs. Point out that tests are like a checklist—making sure all features perform as they should and handle expected edge cases. Bullet Point 3: "Reduce costs" Bugs found early are cheaper to fix. Correcting an issue during development or testing is far less costly compared to fixing it after the product is released. Mention the concept of "shift-left testing," where moving tests earlier in the lifecycle reduces the long-term cost of maintaining the software. Emphasize that saving costs isn't just monetary; it also saves developer time and prevents delays in the release. Bullet Point 4: "Enhance quality" Good testing enhances overall software quality by ensuring reliability and usability. Testing can help maintain consistency throughout updates, making sure that adding a new feature doesn't break existing ones. Mention that quality includes more than just functioning features—it includes the overall user experience, performance, and security of the application. Bullet Point 5: "Build trust" Consistent testing builds trust both internally (within your team) and externally (with users and stakeholders). Talk about how stakeholders are more confident when they know a product is thoroughly tested—it’s proof that you’ve put in effort to deliver a high-quality solution. Relate this to user trust—how a well-tested app leads to fewer issues, resulting in a more reliable product that users can depend on.
