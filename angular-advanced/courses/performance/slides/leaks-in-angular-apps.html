Leaks in Angular apps

A leak: something that lives longer than your component still points at it

  • setInterval and timers
  • listeners on window / document
  • third-party objects: a Leaflet map
  • a manual subscribe to something long-lived

Navigate away ten times: ten components nobody can see, still in memory

Demo: /performance/leaks. Mount a playground card, Unmount, Mount + unmount 10 times: Created 11, Still alive 11; Memory → Collect garbage: still 11. Reload, tick Use the fix, Mount + unmount 10 times, collect garbage: Created 10, Collected 10, Still alive 0. Click: ten components nobody can see. Garbage collection frees what nothing points to. A leak is when something that lives longer than your component still points at it. In a single page app the page lives for hours, so every navigation can add one more. The usual suspects. setInterval: the browser holds your callback, and the callback holds the component. Listeners you put on window or document yourself. Third-party objects like a Leaflet map, which adds its own listener on window. And a subscribe you made by hand to something that outlives the component. Let me show you. /performance/leaks. Mount a card, unmount it, then Mount + unmount 10 times. Created 11, Still alive 11. Open the Memory tab and click Collect garbage: still 11. Nothing lets go of them. Reload, tick Use the fix, same clicks, collect garbage: Created 10, Collected 10, Still alive 0. Click. That's the leak in one sentence: ten components nobody can see, still in memory. Ask the room: where in your own app do you call setInterval or addEventListener, and who stops it? Two of these suspects are in our app on purpose, the footer's interval and the map.
