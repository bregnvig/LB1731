Ask for their two counts first. ~300 -> 5 bars (before 298, 301, 300, 301; after 5, 5, 5). Click: 229 -> 6 ms of change detection in 5 s (median of 3, CPU 4x, Angular track timing). Click: why. Before, each bar: all 6 components (App, Home, Leaflet, Sidebar, SidebarListItem, Footer) and 133 list rows. 6, not 7: PlaygroundDetailComponent only exists on /legeplads/:id. Welcome back. Ask two or three groups for their before and after counts and write them on the board. Ours: about 300 bars in five seconds before, five after. Chrome sends roughly one mousemove per frame while the mouse moves, and every one was a cycle. Your number depends on your mouse and your screen, so the ratio is what counts. Ask the room: what on this page needs to know where the mouse is? Nothing. Leaflet adds its mousemove, drag and zoom listeners when it creates the map, and we created it inside Angular's zone, so zone.js patched them and ran change detection after each one. Created outside the zone, they're never patched, which is why wrapping creation is enough. Click. In milliseconds: with the CPU slowed down four times, like a mid-range phone, change detection took 229 ms of those five seconds before and 6 ms after. That's time the main thread didn't have for anything else. Click. And each of those 300 bars checked all six components and all 133 rows in the sidebar, because a mouse moved over a map. The five left are the footer's one-second timer. The footer bonus: who tried it? The bars went, and so did the counter. The field still changed every second, but nothing told Angular any more. zone.js was the only one telling Angular, and with no zone nobody does. That's exactly what happens to the whole app when we go zoneless, next. If someone asks: the zoom buttons are added after the wrapped call, so clicking them still runs a cycle. That's a click, not a stream.
