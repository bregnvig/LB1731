The bonus is already in the solution, so if you're short on time, open solutions/performance/exercise-4 and read it. First measure. On the home page, run document.querySelectorAll star dot length in the console. We got 1022. Where do they come from? Open one row in the Elements panel: the list item, a button, a div, an h6, a small, another div and a span. Seven, times 133 playgrounds, 931. The list is almost the whole page, and Lighthouse already warns from about 800. Then the change: a styles block on the list item's host, the one on the right. content-visibility auto, and contain-intrinsic-size so the scrollbar doesn't jump while rows are skipped. Ask the room: what will the count be after the change? Click. The same, 1023 against 1022. content-visibility doesn't remove a single node. It tells the browser not to lay out or paint rows that are off screen. If you want to see that, record a reload in the Performance panel before and after and compare the Rendering time; a CPU slowdown makes the difference easier to see. Fewer nodes would be a different fix: fewer elements per row, or virtual scroll.
